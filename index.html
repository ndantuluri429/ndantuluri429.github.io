Neha Dantuluri

MMSc Student in Biomedical Informatics @ Harvard Medical School

I am a biomedical informatics researcher working at the intersection of machine learning, biostatistics, and clinical medicine. My research focuses on building rigorous, interpretable predictive models from electronic health record data to improve patient outcomes.

My graduate thesis, advised by Vesela Kovacheva, MD, PhD and Arjun Manrai, PhD, develops and validates longitudinal ML risk prediction models for stillbirth across ~170,000 pregnancies at Mass General Brigham. I also work in the CAVA Lab at Boston Children's Hospital, evaluating LLM and deep learning performance for fetal acidemia classification using intrapartum electronic fetal monitoring.

Previously, I was an undergraduate summer fellow in the Manrai-Patel Lab at HMS, where I built an interactive R Shiny dashboard integrating national health datasets to model chronic disease measures at the census-tract level. I also conducted geospatial and clinical NLP research in the Marshall Public Health Lab at the University of South Florida.

I hold a B.S. in Information Science (Health Informatics) and a B.S. in Biomedical Science from the University of South Florida. I am supported by the INDECS Full-Tuition Scholarship at Harvard Medical School.

AI/ML for Stillbirth Risk Prediction — Graduate Thesis
Mass General Brigham  ·  PI(s): Vesela Kovacheva, MD, PhD & Arjun Manrai, PhD

Developed and validated longitudinal risk prediction models for stillbirth on ~170,000 pregnancies using penalized and ensemble methods (logistic regression, Elastic Net, gradient boosting, random forest) with 74 computable clinical and longitudinal variables. Designed leakage-safe workflows with training-only MICE imputation; externally validated on the nuMoM2b cohort. Applied SHAP for interpretability.
