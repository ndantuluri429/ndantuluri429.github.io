Neha Dantuluri

MMSc Student in Biomedical Informatics @ Harvard Medical School

I am a biomedical informatics researcher working at the intersection of machine learning, biostatistics, and clinical medicine. My research focuses on building rigorous, interpretable predictive models from electronic health record data to improve patient outcomes.

My graduate thesis, advised by Vesela Kovacheva, MD, PhD and Arjun Manrai, PhD, develops and validates longitudinal ML risk prediction models for stillbirth across ~170,000 pregnancies at Mass General Brigham. I also work in the CAVA Lab at Boston Children's Hospital, evaluating LLM and deep learning performance for fetal acidemia classification using intrapartum electronic fetal monitoring.

Previously, I was an undergraduate summer fellow in the Manrai-Patel Lab at HMS, where I built an interactive R Shiny dashboard integrating national health datasets to model chronic disease measures at the census-tract level. I also conducted geospatial and clinical NLP research in the Marshall Public Health Lab at the University of South Florida.

I hold a B.S. in Information Science (Health Informatics) and a B.S. in Biomedical Science from the University of South Florida. I am supported by the INDECS Full-Tuition Scholarship at Harvard Medical School.

research

ML-Based Stillbirth Risk Prediction
Mass General Brigham  ·  Harvard Medical School PI(s): Vesela Kovacheva, MD, PhD & Arjun Manrai, PhD Aug 2025 – Present
Developed and validated longitudinal risk prediction models for stillbirth on ~170,000 pregnancies using penalized and ensemble methods (logistic regression, Elastic Net, gradient boosting, random forest) with 74 computable clinical and longitudinal variables. Designed leakage-safe workflows with training-only MICE imputation; externally validated on the nuMoM2b cohort. Applied SHAP for interpretability. Graduate thesis research.
AI for Electronic Fetal Monitoring
Boston Children's Hospital  ·  CAVA Lab PI: William La Cava, PhD Sep 2024 – May 2026
Established statistical baselines for physiological time-series; benchmarked zero-shot LLMs against CNN, LSTM, and InceptionTime models for fetal acidemia classification using intrapartum electronic fetal monitoring, with umbilical artery pH as ground truth. Quantified model discrimination (AUROC, balanced accuracy) across multiple pH thresholds against expert obstetrician consensus.
Mobile Health Van Data Resource
Harvard Medical School  ·  Manrai-Patel Lab PI(s): Arjun Manrai, PhD & Chirag Patel, PhD Jun 2023 – Jun 2024
Translated stakeholder requirements from the Family Van and Mobile Health Map into data specifications; built an interactive R Shiny dashboard integrating CDC PLACES, HRSA, and GIS sources to model ~45 chronic disease measures from national to census-tract level. Ran iterative testing and feedback cycles with public health researchers and clinicians.
Maternal Opioid Use Disorder in Florida
University of South Florida  ·  Marshall Public Health Lab PI(s): Jennifer Marshall, PhD & Kimberly Fryer, MD Jan 2022 – Apr 2024
Conducted geospatial analysis and multivariate regression to identify factors associated with maternal opioid use disorder prevalence; findings informed public health strategy. Contributed to a clinical NLP pipeline using ICD-10 codes and text criteria to extract OUD cases from Tampa General Hospital's EHR, supporting clinical surveillance and quality improvement.
Cardiac Pathophysiology Impact of Hyperoxia in Mice Models
University of South Florida PI: Srinivas K. Panguluri, PhD
Contributed to a study examining the influence of age and sex on cardiac electrical, functional, and physical alterations in progressive hyperoxia treatment using a murine model, with a time course study design.
